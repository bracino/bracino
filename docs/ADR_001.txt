# ADR: Prototype BBU Controller – Module-Based Architecture (v0.06)

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-08-12  
**Deciders:** Bracino project  
**Tags:** hardware, prototype, safety-critical, autonomous-control

## Context

The Paradigma MES-BBU controller in the 7-unit condo boiler room near Radicondoli has failed (firmware stuck in manual mode). A replacement is required that:

- Controls a Grundfos ~220 V / ~380 W pump via relay based on tank differential temperatures.
- Provides current telemetry via CT for monitoring.
- Remains fully autonomous (local control loop must work without network).
- Supports a simple local UI (display + rotary encoder).
- Can later report status via ESP-NOW / MQTT (supervisory only).
- Can be built quickly with readily available modules on a prototype board, with enough parts for three units as backups.

Constraints include limited GPIO on the chosen MCU module, the need for proper 220 VAC isolation/creepage on the switched side, and a desire to keep discrete part count low.

## Decision

Adopt a module-centric prototype architecture on a single board (KiCad schematic v0.06):

- **MCU:** Waveshare ESP32-C3-ZERO (provides 5 V in, 3.3 V out, USB-C, limited but sufficient GPIOs).
- **ADC:** ADS1115 4-channel 16-bit I²C module (A0 = CT, A1–A3 = NTC dividers).
- **Current sense:** ZMCT103C module (1000:1, on-board burden + conditioning) powered from 3.3 V.
- **Temperature:** Three 10 kΩ NTCs with 10 kΩ pull-downs forming dividers into the ADS1115.
- **Actuator:** JQC-3FE-S-Z style 5 V relay module (low-level trigger on IN). GPIO10 must not drive IN directly (5 V pull-up on IN; C3 pad is 3.3 V). Planned: NPN or N-FET so GPIO10 high sinks IN to GND (firmware polarity inverts vs v0.06). Module VCC stays 5 V; 3.3 V on the module is a bench workaround only.
- **Snubber:** Dedicated module across the relay contacts containing:
  - 200 Ω 2 W resistor
  - 104J 630 V 100 nF metallized film capacitor
  - 10D471K MOV (470 V)
- **UI:** 1.8″ ST7735S TFT (SPI, CS hard-tied to GND, RESET with 4.7 kΩ external pull-up) + rotary encoder with switch, brought out on dual 1×9 headers for a remote panel.
- **Power:** External 12 V → PPTC (60 V / 0.4 A) → Schottky (1N5819) → bulk capacitor → adjustable buck module set to 5 V. 3.3 V taken from the ESP32-C3-ZERO regulator. Local decoupling capacitors on the rails.
- **Pull-ups:** Rely on MCU internal pull-ups wherever possible (encoder A/B/SW, etc.) to minimise discrete parts.
- **GPIO policy:** No further functional expansion expected. Several pins are strapping or USB-related and treated as off-limits. GPIO10 (relay) also drives the on-board WS2812; the LED is unused in firmware and any incidental illumination when the relay is off is accepted.
- **Design principle:** The control node is fully autonomous. Network features are supervisory only.

Bring-up order (safety-first):

1. Rails only (verify 12 V → 5 V → 3.3 V).
2. Relay + snubber (hand-driven, then small AC load).
3. CT with dummy current (calibration).
4. MCU + relay drive under firmware (no load → small AC load).
5. ADS1115 + NTCs (calibration).
6. UI daughterboard last.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Fast to assemble and debug with modules.
- Clear separation of the 220 VAC zone.
- Sufficient measurement resolution and local UI for the required differential control + telemetry.
- Three complete prototypes possible from existing stock.
- Low discrete part count; easy to rework or replace modules.

### Negative / Accepted risks
- Limited future expansion (SPI CS permanently selected, most GPIOs used or reserved).
- CT output swing is constrained when the ZMCT module is run at 3.3 V. Bench (2026-08-14): path is usable only as on/off — see DESIGN_NOTE_001. Not a calibrated ammeter.
- GPIO10 cannot drive the 5 V relay module IN (pull-up fights the pad and back-feeds 5 V). Transistor interface required; GPIO10 still shares the unused WS2812.
- Mechanical isolation (creepage, slots, potting) on the AC side remains a manual assembly responsibility.
- PCB layout must be refactored to use individual components instead of modules, requiring footprint files, gerbers, etc.

### Neutral
- Further refinements (extra decoupling, soft-start of the relay, exact calibration constants, etc.) deferred until after first-article testing.

## Alternatives considered

- Discrete relay + driver + custom snubber (rejected for speed and simplicity on the prototype).
- Different CT or burden arrangement (ZMCT module preferred for ease of protoboard wiring and known reputation).
- MCP3204 or other SPI ADC (ADS1115 chosen for I²C simplicity and existing module stock).
- Hardware SPI for the TFT (bit-bang accepted given pin constraints and single-device use).
- More elaborate power sequencing or isolated supplies (unnecessary for this scope).
